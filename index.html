The Philippines' Mother Diocese

The Archdiocese of Manila, established in 1579, is one of the oldest and most significant ecclesiastical territories in the Philippines. Throughout its rich history, the Archdiocese of Manila has served as a center of faith, evangelization, and Christian service, guided by generations of bishops and archbishops. Today, it continues its mission of proclaiming the Gospel and serving the Catholic faithful across the six cities of Manila, Makati, Mandaluyong, Pasay, San Juan, and a portion of Taguig City.

OUR MISSION

A people called by the Father in Jesus Christ to become a community of persons with Fullness of Life, witnessing to the Kingdom of God by Living the Paschal Mystery in the Power of the Holy Spirit, with Mary and Joseph as our Companions.

OUR VISION

By 2028, RCAM shall have raised the level of integral faith formation, the promotion of holistic human development, and the stewardship of the Archdiocese and its ecclesial communities to best pastoral practice in the Philippine Church.

OUR HISTORY

The Archdiocese of Manila was established on February 6, 1579, by Pope Gregory XIII, with Fray Domingo de Salazar, OP, as its first bishop. Initially a suffragan diocese under the Archdiocese of Mexico, it was elevated to an archdiocese on August 14, 1595, with three suffragan dioceses: Nueva Cáceres, Nueva Segovia, and Cebu. Under Bishop Salazar, the First Synod of Manila promoted catechism in native languages and recognized the rights of both Christian and non-Christian Filipinos. Throughout its history, the Archdiocese was led by members of various religious congregations and secular clergy. Notable leaders included Archbishop Basilio Sancho, who promoted the formation of native Filipino priests. The Archdiocese experienced significant milestones during the 20th century, including the appointment of Jeremiah Harty as its first non-Spanish archbishop in 1903 and Gabriel M. Reyes as its first Filipino archbishop in 1949. In 1960, Rufino J. Santos became the first Filipino Cardinal Archbishop of Manila. He was followed by Jaime Cardinal Sin, Gaudencio Cardinal Rosales, and Luis Antonio Cardinal Tagle. In 2021, Pope Francis appointed Jose F. Cardinal Advincula as the 33rd Archbishop of Manila, and he was officially installed on June 24, 2021. For more than four centuries, the Archdiocese of Manila has played an important role in the development of Catholicism in the Philippines through missionary work, evangelization, priestly formation, pastoral leadership, and service to the spiritual and social needs of the Filipino faithful.
